Order gelato in Houston after years of supermarket ice cream and the first spoonful can be a surprise. It is denser. The flavor arrives faster and louder. It melts on your tongue instead of sitting there cold. None of that is an accident: gelato is built differently from ice cream, and the differences are a small lesson in food science. Here is what is going on in the cup, and what it means for how we make and serve it at Dubai & Dips in Clear Lake.

## Gelato vs ice cream: the same ingredients, a different balance

Gelato and ice cream start from the same short list: milk, cream, sugar and flavor. What changes is the balance between them, and the way the mix is frozen. Three differences do almost all of the work.

### 1. Less air

Every frozen dessert is churned while it freezes, and churning folds air into the mix. The extra volume that air adds is called **overrun**. Industrial ice cream is often whipped with a lot of air, which is why a tub can feel light and fluffy and why it shrinks so much as it melts.

Gelato is churned more slowly, so less air gets in. Less air means more actual dessert per spoonful: a denser, silkier texture, and more flavor reaching your tongue in every bite.

### 2. Less fat

Gelato is usually made with more milk and less cream than ice cream, so it carries less butterfat. That sounds like a compromise, but it is the opposite. Fat coats the tongue, and a heavy coating mutes flavor. With less of it in the way, pistachio tastes more like pistachio and chocolate more like chocolate.

### 3. A warmer serve

Ice cream is typically served very cold, straight from a deep freezer. Gelato is kept and served a few degrees warmer. At that temperature it stays soft enough to be worked with a paddle rather than scooped into hard balls, and your palate is not numbed by the cold, so you taste more.

Put the three together and you get the gelato signature: dense, smooth, intensely flavored and quick to melt.

## Why texture is really a story about ice crystals

The smoothness of gelato comes down to the size of its ice crystals. Water in the mix freezes into crystals, and the smaller they are, the smoother the texture feels. Big crystals feel grainy or icy.

A few things keep crystals small:

- **Sugar** lowers the freezing point, so not all of the water freezes solid. That is what keeps gelato soft enough to serve.
- **Milk solids** hold onto water so it has less chance to form large crystals.
- **Fast freezing and fresh serving** matter too. Every time a frozen dessert partly melts and refreezes, small crystals merge into bigger ones. That is why gelato is at its best made fresh and served soon after.

This is also why good gelato is a daily job rather than something made in bulk and stored for weeks.

## Gelato in Houston: how to get the best cup

A few tips, whichever shop you visit:

1. **Look at the case.** Gelato should look smooth and glossy, not icy or crusted.
2. **Ask for a taste.** A good shop will happily let you try before you commit.
3. **Eat it soon.** Gelato is served soft and melts faster than ice cream. It is made to be enjoyed right away, not carried home in a hot car.
4. **Pair it.** A scoop next to an espresso drink is a classic. Our [classic coffees](/menu#classic-coffees) include an **Affogato**, where coffee meets something cold and sweet.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between gelato and ice cream?

Gelato usually has less air churned into it, less butterfat, and is served a little warmer than ice cream. The result is denser, smoother and more intensely flavored.

### Why does gelato melt faster than ice cream?

It is served warmer and contains less air and fat, so it softens quickly. That is also why the flavor comes through so clearly.
